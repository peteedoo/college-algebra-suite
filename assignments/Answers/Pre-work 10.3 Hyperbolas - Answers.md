### **Question 1**
* **Problem:** Complete the sentence below: A(n) _______ is the collection of points in the plane, the difference of whose distances from two fixed points is a constant.
* **Answer:** **`hyperbola`**
* **The Rule:** A hyperbola is the set of all points in a plane such that the absolute difference of their distances from two fixed points (the foci) is constant.
* **Work:**
  1. Whereas an ellipse keeps the *sum* of distances from two fixed points constant, a **hyperbola** keeps the *difference* of distances constant.

### **Question 2**
* **Problem:** Fill in the blank with the most appropriate term or phrase: The line segment between the vertices of the hyperbola is called the (1) _______ of the hyperbola.
* **Answer:** **`transverse axis`**
* **The Rule:** The line segment connecting the two vertices of a hyperbola is the **transverse axis** (of length $2a$), while the perpendicular segment of length $2b$ through the center is the conjugate axis.
* **Work:**
  1. Major and minor axes apply to ellipses; for a hyperbola, the segment joining the vertices is the **transverse axis**.

### **Question 3**
* **Problem:** Fill in the blanks so that the resulting statement is true: The set of all points in a plane the difference of whose distances from two fixed points is constant is a/an (1) _______. The two fixed points are called the (2) _______. The line through these points intersects the graph at two points, called the (3) _______, which are joined by the (4) _______ axis.
* **Answer:**
  * **(1):** **`hyperbola.`**
  * **(2):** **`foci.`**
  * **(3):** **`vertices,`**
  * **(4):** **`transverse`**
* **The Rule:** A hyperbola is defined by two fixed points (**foci**), and the line through the foci intersects the branches at the **vertices**, which are connected by the **transverse** axis.
* **Work:**
  1. Constant difference of distances $\rightarrow$ **hyperbola**.
  2. Two fixed points $\rightarrow$ **foci**.
  3. Points where the focal axis intersects the hyperbola $\rightarrow$ **vertices**.
  4. Segment joining the vertices $\rightarrow$ **transverse** axis.

### **Question 4**
* **Problem:** Fill in the blanks so that the resulting statement is true: The two branches of the graph of a hyperbola approach a pair of intersecting lines, called (1) _______. These intersecting lines pass through the (2) _______ of the hyperbola.
* **Answer:**
  * **(1):** **`asymptotes.`**
  * **(2):** **`center`**
* **The Rule:** Every hyperbola has two intersecting slant **asymptotes** that guide its branches and intersect at the **center** $(h, k)$ of the hyperbola.
* **Work:**
  1. The guiding lines approached by the branches as $|x|, |y| \to \infty$ are the **asymptotes**.
  2. The diagonals of the fundamental rectangle (the asymptotes) cross at the **center** of the hyperbola.

* **Answer:** **Choice C** (`The standard form of a horizontal hyperbola centered at the origin is x^2/a^2 - y^2/b^2 = 1 with a and b being positive real numbers.`)

* **Work:**

### **Question 6**
* **Problem:** Match each equation in Column I with the correct graph in Column II:
  * $\frac{x^2}{36} + \frac{y^2}{25} = 1$
  * $\frac{x^2}{25} + \frac{y^2}{36} = 1$
  * $\frac{x^2}{25} - \frac{y^2}{36} = 1$
  * $\frac{x^2}{36} - \frac{y^2}{25} = 1$
* **Answer:**
  * $\frac{x^2}{36} + \frac{y^2}{25} = 1 \rightarrow$ **`A`** (Horizontal ellipse with intercepts $(\pm 6, 0)$ and $(0, \pm 5)$)
  * $\frac{x^2}{25} + \frac{y^2}{36} = 1 \rightarrow$ **`C`** (Vertical ellipse with intercepts $(\pm 5, 0)$ and $(0, \pm 6)$)
  * $\frac{x^2}{25} - \frac{y^2}{36} = 1 \rightarrow$ **`B`** (Horizontal hyperbola with vertices $(\pm 5, 0)$ and steeper asymptotes $y = \pm \frac{6}{5}x$)
  * $\frac{x^2}{36} - \frac{y^2}{25} = 1 \rightarrow$ **`D`** (Horizontal hyperbola with vertices $(\pm 6, 0)$ and flatter asymptotes $y = \pm \frac{5}{6}x$)
* **Graph Equation:** `x^2/36 - y^2/25 = 1`
* **The Rule:** Equations with a $+$ sign between squared terms are ellipses; equations with a $-$ sign are hyperbolas with vertices at $(\pm a, 0)$ and asymptotes $y = \pm \frac{b}{a}x$.
* **Work:**
  1. $\frac{x^2}{36} + \frac{y^2}{25} = 1$ is a horizontal ellipse ($a=6 > b=5$) $\rightarrow$ **A**.
  2. $\frac{x^2}{25} + \frac{y^2}{36} = 1$ is a vertical ellipse ($b=5 < a=6$) $\rightarrow$ **C**.
  3. $\frac{x^2}{25} - \frac{y^2}{36} = 1$ has vertices $(\pm 5, 0)$ and asymptote slope $\pm \frac{6}{5}$ (crosses $y = \pm 10$ before $x = \pm 10$) $\rightarrow$ **B**.
  4. $\frac{x^2}{36} - \frac{y^2}{25} = 1$ has vertices $(\pm 6, 0)$ and asymptote slope $\pm \frac{5}{6}$ (crosses $x = \pm 10$ before $y = \pm 10$) $\rightarrow$ **D**.

### **Question 10**
* **Problem:** Find the standard form of the equation of the hyperbola shown (horizontal hyperbola centered at $(0,0)$ with vertices at $(\pm 1, 0)$ and fundamental box corners at $(\pm 1, \pm 2)$).
* **Answer:** **`x^2 - y^2/4 = 1`**
* **Graph Equation:** `x^2/1 - y^2/4 = 1`
* **The Rule:** Read the horizontal vertex distance $a$ and vertical box height $b$ from the graph and substitute into $\frac{x^2}{a^2} - \frac{y^2}{b^2} = 1$.
* **Work:**
  1. The hyperbola opens left and right with vertices at $(-1, 0)$ and $(1, 0)$, so $a = 1 \implies a^2 = 1$.
  2. The fundamental rectangle extends vertically to $y = \pm 2$ (and asymptotes pass through $(1, 2)$ and $(5, 10)$), so $b = 2 \implies b^2 = 4$.
  3. Standard form equation: $x^2 - \frac{y^2}{4} = 1$.

### **Question 11**
* **Problem:** Find the standard form of the equation of the hyperbola shown (vertical hyperbola centered at $(0,0)$ with vertices at $(0, \pm 4)$ and asymptotes passing through $(\pm 3, \pm 4)$ and $(\pm 6, \pm 8)$).
* **Answer:** **`y^2/16 - x^2/9 = 1`**
* **Graph Equation:** `y^2/16 - x^2/9 = 1`
* **The Rule:** For a vertical hyperbola centered at $(0,0)$ with vertices $(0, \pm a)$ and asymptote slope $\pm \frac{a}{b}$, the equation is $\frac{y^2}{a^2} - \frac{x^2}{b^2} = 1$.
* **Work:**
  1. The vertices are on the $y$-axis at $(0, 4)$ and $(0, -4)$, so $a = 4 \implies a^2 = 16$.
  2. The asymptote passes through $(0,0)$ and $(3,4)$ (and $(6,8)$), so $\frac{a}{b} = \frac{4}{3} \implies b = 3 \implies b^2 = 9$.
  3. Standard form equation: $\frac{y^2}{16} - \frac{x^2}{9} = 1$.

### **Question 12**
* **Problem:** Graph the relation $\frac{y^2}{4} - \frac{x^2}{16} = 1$. Use the relation's graph to determine its domain and range.
  * **a.** Which of the following is the graph of $\frac{y^2}{4} - \frac{x^2}{16} = 1$?
  * **b.** What is the domain of $\frac{y^2}{4} - \frac{x^2}{16} = 1$?
  * **c.** What is the range of $\frac{y^2}{4} - \frac{x^2}{16} = 1$?
* **Answer:**
  * **a:** **Choice C**
  * **b (Domain):** **`(-\infty,\infty)`**
  * **c (Range):** **`(-\infty,-2] \cup [2,\infty)`**
* **Graph Equation:** `y^2/4 - x^2/16 = 1`
* **The Rule:** A vertical hyperbola $\frac{y^2}{a^2} - \frac{x^2}{b^2} = 1$ opens up and down from $(0, \pm a)$, has domain $(-\infty, \infty)$, and has range $(-\infty, -a] \cup [a, \infty)$.
* **Work:**
  1. **a.** Since $y^2$ is positive with $a = \sqrt{4} = 2$ and $b = \sqrt{16} = 4$, the graph is a vertical hyperbola with vertices at $(0, \pm 2)$, which is **Choice C**.
  2. **b.** The branches extend infinitely left and right, so the domain is $(-\infty, \infty)$.
  3. **c.** Since $\frac{y^2}{4} = 1 + \frac{x^2}{16} \ge 1 \implies y^2 \ge 4 \implies |y| \ge 2$, the range is $(-\infty, -2] \cup [2, \infty)$.

### **Question 13**
* **Problem:** Fill in the blanks so that the resulting statement is true: The equation $49x^2 - 9y^2 = 441$ can be written in standard form by (1) _______ both sides by (2) _______.
* **Answer:**
  * **(1):** **`dividing`**
  * **(2):** **`441.`**
* **Graph Equation:** `x^2/9 - y^2/49 = 1`
* **The Rule:** Standard form of a hyperbola requires the right-hand side to equal $1$, which is achieved by dividing both sides by the constant on the right.
* **Work:**
  1. To turn $441$ on the right-hand side into $1$, we **divide** both sides by **$441$** to get $\frac{x^2}{9} - \frac{y^2}{49} = 1$.

### **Question 15**
* **Problem:** Fill in the blank so that the resulting statement is true: The center of $\frac{(y+8)^2}{4} - \frac{(x-8)^2}{16} = 1$ is _______.
* **Answer:** **`(8,-8)`**
* **Graph Equation:** `(y+8)^2/4 - (x-8)^2/16 = 1`
* **The Rule:** Regardless of which term comes first, the $x$-coordinate of the center $h$ comes from $(x-h)^2$ and the $y$-coordinate $k$ comes from $(y-k)^2$.
* **Work:**
  1. From $(x-8)^2$, the $x$-coordinate of the center is $h = 8$.
  2. From $(y+8)^2 = (y - (-8))^2$, the $y$-coordinate of the center is $k = -8$.
  3. Thus, the center is $(h, k) = (8, -8)$.

### **Question 16**
* **Problem:** Match each equation of a hyperbola in Column I with its description in Column II:
  * $\frac{(x-2)^2}{36} - \frac{(y-1)^2}{64} = 1$
  * $\frac{(x+2)^2}{64} - \frac{(y+1)^2}{36} = 1$
  * $\frac{(y-2)^2}{9} - \frac{(x-1)^2}{25} = 1$
  * $\frac{(y+2)^2}{25} - \frac{(x+1)^2}{9} = 1$
* **Answer:**
  * $\frac{(x-2)^2}{36} - \frac{(y-1)^2}{64} = 1 \rightarrow$ **`A`** (`center (2,1); horizontal transverse axis`)
  * $\frac{(x+2)^2}{64} - \frac{(y+1)^2}{36} = 1 \rightarrow$ **`B`** (`center (-2,-1); horizontal transverse axis`)
  * $\frac{(y-2)^2}{9} - \frac{(x-1)^2}{25} = 1 \rightarrow$ **`D`** (`center (1,2); vertical transverse axis`)
  * $\frac{(y+2)^2}{25} - \frac{(x+1)^2}{9} = 1 \rightarrow$ **`C`** (`center (-1,-2); vertical transverse axis`)
* **Graph Equation:** `(x-2)^2/36 - (y-1)^2/64 = 1`
* **The Rule:** If $(x-h)^2$ is positive, the transverse axis is horizontal; if $(y-k)^2$ is positive, the transverse axis is vertical. The center is always $(h, k)$.
* **Work:**
  1. $\frac{(x-2)^2}{36} - \frac{(y-1)^2}{64} = 1$: $(x-h)^2$ first $\implies$ horizontal, center $(2, 1) \rightarrow$ **A**.
  2. $\frac{(x+2)^2}{64} - \frac{(y+1)^2}{36} = 1$: $(x-h)^2$ first $\implies$ horizontal, center $(-2, -1) \rightarrow$ **B**.
  3. $\frac{(y-2)^2}{9} - \frac{(x-1)^2}{25} = 1$: $(y-k)^2$ first $\implies$ vertical, center $(h, k) = (1, 2) \rightarrow$ **D**.
  4. $\frac{(y+2)^2}{25} - \frac{(x+1)^2}{9} = 1$: $(y-k)^2$ first $\implies$ vertical, center $(h, k) = (-1, -2) \rightarrow$ **C**.
