### **Question 1**
* **Problem:** Explain how to draw the graph of the hyperbola $\frac{x^2}{36} - \frac{y^2}{4} = 1$.
* **Answer:** **Choice C** (`This is a horizontal hyperbola, centered at the origin, with vertices (6,0) and (-6,0). Draw a fundamental rectangle with corners at (6,2), (6,-2), (-6,2), and (-6,-2). Extend the diagonals of the rectangle as asymptotes of the hyperbola. Construct each branch of the hyperbola passing through a vertex and approaching the asymptotes.`)
* **Graph Equation:** `x^2/36 - y^2/4 = 1`
* **The Rule:** For $\frac{x^2}{a^2} - \frac{y^2}{b^2} = 1$, the hyperbola is horizontal with vertices $(\pm a, 0)$ and fundamental rectangle corners $(\pm a, \pm b)$.
* **Work:**
  1. Since $x^2$ is positive, the hyperbola is horizontal with $a = \sqrt{36} = 6$ and $b = \sqrt{4} = 2$.
  2. The vertices are $(6, 0)$ and $(-6, 0)$, and the fundamental rectangle has corners $(\pm 6, \pm 2)$, matching **Choice C**.

### **Question 2**
* **Problem:** Complete the sentence below: For the hyperbola $\frac{y^2}{16} - \frac{x^2}{81} = 1$, the asymptotes are (1) _______.
* **Answer:** **`y = -4/9 x and y = 4/9 x.`** (Third option: $y = -\frac{4}{9}x \text{ and } y = \frac{4}{9}x$)
* **Graph Equation:** `y^2/16 - x^2/81 = 1`
* **The Rule:** For a vertical hyperbola $\frac{y^2}{a^2} - \frac{x^2}{b^2} = 1$, the asymptotes are $y = \pm \frac{a}{b}x$ (vertical rise $a$ over horizontal run $b$).
* **Work:**
  1. Identify $a = \sqrt{16} = 4$ (under $y^2$) and $b = \sqrt{81} = 9$ (under $x^2$).
  2. The asymptotes are $y = -\frac{4}{9}x$ and $y = \frac{4}{9}x$.

### **Question 6**
* **Problem:** What can be said about the hyperbola with equation $\frac{x^2}{a^2} - \frac{y^2}{b^2} = 1$? Select all that apply.
* **Answer:** **Choices A, B, E**

  * **B.** `Its center is at (0,0).`
  * **E.** `It has a horizontal transverse axis.`
* **Graph Equation:** `x^2/16 - y^2/9 = 1`
* **The Rule:** $\frac{x^2}{a^2} - \frac{y^2}{b^2} = 1$ is centered at $(0,0)$, has a horizontal transverse axis along the $x$-axis, and has vertices at $(\pm a, 0)$.
* **Work:**
  1. Since there are no $(x-h)$ or $(y-k)$ shifts, the center is $(0,0)$ (**B**).
  2. Because $x^2$ is the positive term, the transverse axis is horizontal (**E**) and the vertices are $(\pm a, 0)$ (**A**).

### **Question 13**
* **Problem:** Graph the hyperbola $\frac{(y-8)^2}{81} - \frac{(x-4)^2}{144} = 1$. Give the domain, range, center, vertices, and equations of the asymptotes for the figure.
* **Answer:**
  * **Center:** **`(4,8)`**

  * **Asymptotes:** **Choice C** (`y = \pm \frac{3}{4}(x-4) + 8`)

### **Question 15**
* **Problem:** Find an equation for the hyperbola whose graph is below (horizontal hyperbola with asymptotes intersecting at $(-1, -3)$ and vertices at $(-4, -3)$ and $(2, -3)$).
* **Answer:** **`(x+1)^2/9 - (y+3)^2/4 = 1`**
* **Graph Equation:** `(x+1)^2/9 - (y+3)^2/4 = 1`
* **The Rule:** Find $(h, k)$ where the asymptotes intersect, $a$ from the distance to the vertices, and $b$ from the vertical rise of the asymptote over run $a$.
* **Work:**
  1. The asymptotes intersect at the center $(h, k) = (-1, -3)$.
  2. The vertices are at $(-4, -3)$ and $(2, -3)$, so the hyperbola is horizontal with $a = 2 - (-1) = 3 \implies a^2 = 9$.
  3. Moving $a = 3$ units right from $(-1, -3)$ to $x = 2$, the asymptotes rise/fall by $b = 2$ units to $(2, -1)$ and $(2, -5)$, so $b = 2 \implies b^2 = 4$.
  4. Standard form equation: $\frac{(x+1)^2}{9} - \frac{(y+3)^2}{4} = 1$.

### **Question 16**
* **Problem:** Find the equation of a hyperbola satisfying the given conditions: vertices at $(0,9)$ and $(0,-9)$; asymptotes $y = \pm \frac{3}{2}x$.
* **Answer:** **Choice B** (`y^2/81 - x^2/36 = 1`)
* **Graph Equation:** `y^2/81 - x^2/36 = 1`
* **The Rule:** Vertices at $(0, \pm a)$ give a vertical hyperbola $\frac{y^2}{a^2} - \frac{x^2}{b^2} = 1$ with asymptote slope $\frac{a}{b}$.

  3. Equation: $\frac{y^2}{81} - \frac{x^2}{36} = 1$ (**Choice B**).

### **Question 19**
* **Problem:** A hyperbola's center is not at the origin. Its vertices are $(8,-14)$ and $(8,0)$. One asymptote is $y = -\frac{7}{8}x$. Find the equation of the hyperbola.
* **Answer:** **`(y+7)^2/49 - (x-8)^2/64 = 1`**
* **Graph Equation:** `(y+7)^2/49 - (x-8)^2/64 = 1`
* **The Rule:** Find the center $(h, k)$ as the midpoint of the vertices; since the $x$-coordinates of the vertices match, the hyperbola is vertical with $\frac{(y-k)^2}{a^2} - \frac{(x-h)^2}{b^2} = 1$ and asymptotes $y - k = \pm \frac{a}{b}(x - h)$.
* **Work:**
  1. Center $(h, k)$ is the midpoint of $(8, -14)$ and $(8, 0)$: $(h, k) = \left(8, \frac{-14 + 0}{2}\right) = (8, -7)$.
  2. Since the vertices lie on the vertical line $x = 8$, the transverse axis is vertical with $a = 0 - (-7) = 7 \implies a^2 = 49$ (under $(y+7)^2$).
  3. The asymptote $y = -\frac{7}{8}x$ can be rewritten as $y + 7 = -\frac{7}{8}(x - 8)$, so $\frac{a}{b} = \frac{7}{8} \implies \frac{7}{b} = \frac{7}{8} \implies b = 8 \implies b^2 = 64$ (under $(x-8)^2$).
  4. Equation: $\frac{(y+7)^2}{49} - \frac{(x-8)^2}{64} = 1$.
