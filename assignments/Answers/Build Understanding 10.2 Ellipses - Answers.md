### **Question 1**
* **Problem:** Match each equation of an ellipse in Column I with the appropriate intercepts in Column II.
  * $64x^2 + 9y^2 = 576$
  * $9x^2 + 64y^2 = 576$
  * $\frac{x^2}{36} + \frac{y^2}{4} = 1$
  * $\frac{x^2}{4} + \frac{y^2}{36} = 1$
* **Answer:**
  * $64x^2 + 9y^2 = 576 \rightarrow$ **`C`** (`(-3,0), (3,0), (0,-8), (0,8)`)
  * $9x^2 + 64y^2 = 576 \rightarrow$ **`A`** (`(-8,0), (8,0), (0,-3), (0,3)`)
  * $\frac{x^2}{36} + \frac{y^2}{4} = 1 \rightarrow$ **`D`** (`(-6,0), (6,0), (0,-2), (0,2)`)
  * $\frac{x^2}{4} + \frac{y^2}{36} = 1 \rightarrow$ **`B`** (`(-2,0), (2,0), (0,-6), (0,6)`)
* **Graph Equation:** `x^2/9 + y^2/64 = 1`
* **The Rule:** Put each equation into standard form $\frac{x^2}{b^2} + \frac{y^2}{a^2} = 1$; the $x$-intercepts are $(\pm\sqrt{\text{denom of } x^2}, 0)$ and the $y$-intercepts are $(0, \pm\sqrt{\text{denom of } y^2})$.
* **Work:**
  1. Divide $64x^2 + 9y^2 = 576$ by $576$: $\frac{x^2}{9} + \frac{y^2}{64} = 1 \implies x$-intercepts $(\pm 3, 0)$, $y$-intercepts $(0, \pm 8) \rightarrow$ **C**.
  2. Divide $9x^2 + 64y^2 = 576$ by $576$: $\frac{x^2}{64} + \frac{y^2}{9} = 1 \implies x$-intercepts $(\pm 8, 0)$, $y$-intercepts $(0, \pm 3) \rightarrow$ **A**.
  3. For $\frac{x^2}{36} + \frac{y^2}{4} = 1$: $x$-intercepts $(\pm 6, 0)$, $y$-intercepts $(0, \pm 2) \rightarrow$ **D**.
  4. For $\frac{x^2}{4} + \frac{y^2}{36} = 1$: $x$-intercepts $(\pm 2, 0)$, $y$-intercepts $(0, \pm 6) \rightarrow$ **B**.

### **Question 2**
* **Problem:** Determine whether or not each equation is that of an ellipse. If it is not, state the kind of graph the equation has:
  * (a) $x^2 + 16y^2 = 16$
  * (b) $x^2 + y^2 = 16$
  * (c) $x^2 + y = 16$
  * (d) $\frac{x}{16} + \frac{y}{25} = 1$
* **Answer:**
  * **(a):** **Choice D** (`It is an ellipse.`)
  * **(b):** **Choice C** (`It is not an ellipse. It is a circle.`)
  * **(c):** **Choice A** (`It is not an ellipse. It is a parabola.`)
  * **(d):** **Choice C** (`It is not an ellipse. It is a line.`)
* **Graph Equation:** `x^2/16 + y^2/1 = 1`
* **The Rule:** An ellipse requires both $x^2$ and $y^2$ terms with positive, unequal coefficients ($A x^2 + C y^2 = D$ with $A \neq C$ and $A, C, D > 0$).
* **Work:**
  1. **(a)** Dividing by $16$ gives $\frac{x^2}{16} + \frac{y^2}{1} = 1$, which is an **ellipse** (**Choice D**).
  2. **(b)** Both squared terms have equal coefficients ($r^2 = 16$), which is a **circle** (**Choice C**).
  3. **(c)** Only $x$ is squared ($y = -x^2 + 16$), which is a **parabola** (**Choice A**).
  4. **(d)** Both $x$ and $y$ are to the first power, which is a **line** (**Choice C**).

### **Question 3**
* **Problem:** For the ellipse $\frac{x^2}{64} + \frac{y^2}{4} = 1$, give the domain, range, center, vertices, and foci.
* **Answer:**
  * **Domain:** **`[-8,8]`**
  * **Range:** **`[-2,2]`**
  * **Center:** **`(0,0)`**
  * **Vertices:** **`(-8,0), (8,0)`**
  * **Foci:** **`(-2\sqrt{15},0), (2\sqrt{15},0)`**
* **Graph Equation:** `x^2/64 + y^2/4 = 1`
* **The Rule:** For a horizontal ellipse $\frac{x^2}{a^2} + \frac{y^2}{b^2} = 1$ ($a > b$), domain is $[-a, a]$, range is $[-b, b]$, vertices are $(\pm a, 0)$, and foci are $(\pm c, 0)$ where $c^2 = a^2 - b^2$.
* **Work:**
  1. Here $a^2 = 64 \implies a = 8$ and $b^2 = 4 \implies b = 2$, centered at $(0,0)$.
  2. Domain: $[-8, 8]$; Range: $[-2, 2]$.
  3. Major axis is horizontal, so vertices are $(-8, 0)$ and $(8, 0)$.
  4. $c = \sqrt{a^2 - b^2} = \sqrt{64 - 4} = \sqrt{60} = 2\sqrt{15}$, giving foci $(-2\sqrt{15}, 0)$ and $(2\sqrt{15}, 0)$.

### **Question 14**
* **Problem:** Find the standard form of the equation of the ellipse satisfying the following conditions: Vertices of major axis are $(-4, 8)$ and $(-4, -4)$. The length of the minor axis is $8$.
* **Answer:** **`(x+4)^2/16 + (y-2)^2/36 = 1`**
* **Graph Equation:** `(x+4)^2/16 + (y-2)^2/36 = 1`
* **The Rule:** The center $(h, k)$ is the midpoint of the vertices, $2a$ is the distance between the vertices, and $2b$ is the minor axis length.
* **Work:**
  1. Midpoint of $(-4, 8)$ and $(-4, -4)$: $(h, k) = \left(-4, \frac{8 + (-4)}{2}\right) = (-4, 2)$.
  2. Since the $x$-coordinates of the vertices are equal, the major axis is vertical with $a = 8 - 2 = 6 \implies a^2 = 36$ (under $(y-2)^2$).
  3. Minor axis length $2b = 8 \implies b = 4 \implies b^2 = 16$ (under $(x+4)^2$).
  4. Equation: $\frac{(x+4)^2}{16} + \frac{(y-2)^2}{36} = 1$.

### **Question 17**
* **Problem:** Draftspeople often use the method shown in the sketch to draw an ellipse. Why does this method work?
* **Answer:** **Choice C** (`Fixing a string to two points (foci) and sweeping a curve as shown will sketch an ellipse, because the sum of the distances from the two fixed points to the pencil's tip remains constant.`)
* **The Rule:** A taut string of fixed length pinned at two foci ensures $d_1 + d_2 = \text{length of string} = \text{constant}$, which is the geometric definition of an ellipse.
* **Work:**
  1. The two pins represent the **foci** (not the vertices).
  2. Because the string's total length does not change, the sum of the distances from the two foci to the pencil tip is constant (**Choice C**).

### **Question 18**
* **Problem:** A one-way road passes under an overpass in the shape of half an ellipse, $15\text{ ft}$ high at the center and $20\text{ ft}$ wide. Assuming a truck is $12\text{ ft}$ wide, what is the tallest truck that can pass under the overpass?
* **Answer:** **`12`** ft tall
* **Graph Equation:** `x^2/100 + y^2/225 = 1`
* **The Rule:** Model the arch with an ellipse centered at $(0,0)$ having horizontal width $2b = 20$ ($b = 10$) and vertical height $a = 15$, then evaluate $y$ at $x = 6$.
* **Work:**
  1. The ellipse equation is $\frac{x^2}{10^2} + \frac{y^2}{15^2} = 1 \implies \frac{x^2}{100} + \frac{y^2}{225} = 1$.
  2. A $12\text{ ft}$ wide truck centered on the road reaches $x = \pm 6\text{ ft}$ from the center.
  3. Substitute $x = 6$: $\frac{6^2}{100} + \frac{y^2}{225} = 1 \implies \frac{36}{100} + \frac{y^2}{225} = 1 \implies \frac{y^2}{225} = \frac{64}{100}$.
  4. Solve for $y > 0$: $y^2 = 225 \cdot \frac{64}{100} = \frac{9 \cdot 64}{4} = 9 \cdot 16 = 144 \implies y = 12\text{ ft}$.

### **Question 19**
* **Problem:** A lithotripter is based on an ellipse with the equation $\frac{x^2}{16} + \frac{y^2}{9} = 1$. Find the distance from the ellipse's center to the kidney stone and to the beam source.
* **Answer:** **`\sqrt{7}`**
* **Graph Equation:** `x^2/16 + y^2/9 = 1`
* **The Rule:** Both the beam source and the kidney stone are located at the foci of the ellipse, which are at distance $c = \sqrt{a^2 - b^2}$ from the center.
* **Work:**
  1. From $\frac{x^2}{16} + \frac{y^2}{9} = 1$, we have $a^2 = 16$ and $b^2 = 9$.
  2. Calculate $c$: $c^2 = a^2 - b^2 = 16 - 9 = 7 \implies c = \sqrt{7}$.
  3. Therefore, the distance from the center to the kidney stone and to the beam source is $\sqrt{7}$.
